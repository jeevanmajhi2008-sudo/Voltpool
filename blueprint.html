The idea, the pros and cons, the full technical picture behind it, and the exact plan to ship the web MVP in 14 days.

Short verdict

A strong, focused MVP idea. The problem (too few charging points) is real, the model is easy to explain, and the core flow can be fully demonstrated as a web app. Version 2 borrows what works from two proven businesses. From Airbnb come trust, choice and hosts earning from something they already own. From Uber and Rapido come speed: one tap to get matched, live tracking, a start PIN, and peak pricing. The hard parts are still not the software. They are getting enough hosts in one area, trusting the energy reading, and keeping drivers from paying hosts directly.

How the v3 features work technically

PieceHow it is built (production)
Two portals, one backendOne API with role-based access (driver, host, admin). Each app subscribes to a WebSocket channel per booking, so a state change by one side pushes to the other in under a second. Bookings follow one state machine: negotiating → requested → confirmed → enroute → arrived → charging → done, with declined and cancelled exits.
Camera and photosWeb: <input type="file" accept="image/*" capture="environment"> opens the phone camera with no app install. Resize in the browser (canvas, ~1280 px JPEG), upload straight to object storage with a pre-signed URL (AWS S3 Mumbai or Cloudinary), strip EXIF location for privacy, and run an automatic check (e.g. AWS Rekognition) to block unsafe images. Check-in photos are kept with the session as dispute evidence.
Pricing enginePrice = base × time-band multiplier × demand multiplier, clamped by a platform floor and ceiling. Demand level per H3 cell = drivers searching ÷ free chargers over the last 15 minutes, computed every few minutes and cached in Redis. The price is locked when the booking is confirmed so it cannot change mid-session.
NegotiationA price_offers row per round with an expiry (e.g. 3 minutes). Rules: offer must be 70–100% of the current price; at most 2 counters; the host's auto-accept rule runs server-side instantly. Accepting re-computes the wallet hold at the agreed price.
NotificationsAn event bus (booking events) feeds a notification service that writes an in-app inbox row and sends push via Firebase Cloud Messaging, with WhatsApp or SMS fallback for critical ones (request waiting, driver arrived). Users choose which types they get.
Offers engineOffers table with audience (driver / host), time windows, demand conditions, caps and budgets. Driver coupons are paid by VoltPool, so host payouts never drop. Host offers change the commission rate or add a bonus, recorded per session for clean accounting.

1. Pros and cons

Pros

  • Real gap. India has far fewer public chargers than its growing EV fleet needs, and range anxiety is a top reason people delay buying an EV.
  • Asset-light. Hosts already own the charger or socket, so you build a network without capex.
  • Hosts earn on an idle asset. A home charger sits unused most of the day.
  • Huge, cheap 2-wheeler and 3-wheeler segment. A shop with an earthed 15A socket can become a host on day one.
  • Shop hosts get extra footfall: drivers buy coffee or groceries while they charge.
  • Familiar patterns. Indians already trust "book a stay" and "book a ride" apps, so there is little to learn.
  • Several revenue lines: session commission, van rescues, and later a driver pass and featured listings.

Cons

  • Chicken-and-egg. Drivers need hosts nearby and hosts need drivers. You must launch one neighbourhood at a time.
  • Trust and safety: strangers on private property, damage, overstaying. Needs KYC, ratings and a deposit.
  • Metering. Without a smart charger or plug, the kWh billed is an estimate, which invites disputes.
  • Small ticket size. A scooter session earns ₹4–5 in commission, so you need volume.
  • Electricity rules. Charging strangers from a normal domestic meter is a grey area in some states. See section 9.
  • Leakage. A regular driver may start paying the host directly after the first visit.
  • The van service needs vehicles, staff and portable chargers, so start it with one partner, not your own fleet.

2. The Airbnb and Uber blend

VoltPool sits between the two models. The supply side (hosts and their chargers) behaves like Airbnb: fixed places, chosen and trusted through listings and reviews. The demand side (a driver who needs charge now) behaves like Uber or Rapido: urgent, location-based, and solved with matching and live tracking.

3. The full technical picture

Listing and discovery

  • Listing. Host adds location (lat/lng), connector, power (kW), base price per kWh, hours, amenities, photos, instant book, smart pricing and cancellation policy. Indian connectors to support: 15A socket, Bharat AC-001, Type 2 AC, CCS2 DC, LECCS.
  • Geo-search. Store points in Postgres with PostGIS and query with ST_DWithin for "chargers within 5 km". At bigger scale, index by H3 hexagons or Redis GEOSEARCH for millisecond lookups.
  • Search ranking. Compatible plug first, then a score from distance, price, rating, availability and host reply rate.

Matching ("Charge now")

  • Filter: active, free now, plug fits the driver's EV, open at this hour.
  • Score each candidate: score = 1.2 × km + 0.25 × ₹/kWh − 1.5 × rating + busy penalty (the demo uses this formula). Lowest score wins. Later, learn the weights from which matches drivers accept.
  • Offer the best match with a short hold (for example 60 seconds) so two drivers are not given the same charger at the same time. Use a Redis lock or a database row lock.

Live tracking and ETA

  • The driver's phone sends GPS every few seconds while a booking is "on the way". The server pushes updates to the host over WebSockets (Socket.IO, Ably or Supabase Realtime).
  • ETA comes from a routing service: Google Distance Matrix, Mapbox, or free OSRM on OpenStreetMap data. The demo estimates it from distance at city speed.
  • Rescue vans use the same pipeline in reverse: the van streams its location to the driver.

Booking state machine

Every booking moves through fixed states, saved with timestamps so disputes can be checked later: requested → confirmed → en_route → arrived → charging → completed, with cancelled and no_show as exits. Each change triggers a notification and is only allowed from the right previous state.

Start PIN

  • When a booking is confirmed, the server generates a random 4-digit PIN and stores only its hash. The driver sees the PIN; the host enters it in the host app. A match moves the booking to "charging".
  • With smart chargers later, the PIN or a QR scan can unlock the charger directly through OCPP RemoteStartTransaction.

Pricing

  • price = base × time multiplier × demand multiplier, capped (for example at 1.5×). Time multiplier in the demo: 1.2 in the evening peak (6–10 pm), 0.85 at night (11 pm–6 am).
  • Demand multiplier later: open booking requests ÷ free chargers in the same H3 cell over the last 15 minutes.
  • Always show the price before booking and lock it for that booking, so drivers never get a surprise.

Metering

  • Level 1 (MVP): energy = (target % − current %) × battery kWh ÷ 0.92, confirmed by both sides.
  • Level 2: a Wi-Fi smart plug or energy meter reports real kWh to the API.
  • Level 3: OCPP 1.6J or 2.0.1 smart chargers send start, stop and MeterValues over WebSocket to your OCPP server.

Payments, wallet and promos

  • Holds. Block an estimated amount (110% of the estimate) at booking with a UPI one-time mandate or card pre-authorisation, then capture only the real amount when charging ends.
  • Split payouts. Razorpay Route or Cashfree Easy Split sends 85% to the host's bank or UPI and keeps 15%. Payouts can run weekly.
  • Wallet. Keep a double-entry ledger (every rupee has a debit and a credit line), never just a balance number. Holding prepaid customer money needs a payment partner's licensed wallet or RBI PPI rules, so use the gateway's product rather than building your own.
  • Promos. A rules table: code, discount type, cap, conditions (first charge, night only, minimum amount), usage limit per user. The platform pays for the discount, so the host's payout does not change.

Chat, notifications and trust

  • Chat over WebSockets, stored per booking. Calls go through number masking (for example Exotel), so neither side sees the other's phone number.
  • Notifications: push (Firebase Cloud Messaging), SMS, and WhatsApp Business API for booking confirmations and "charging finished".
  • KYC: phone OTP for everyone; DigiLocker or Aadhaar-based eKYC through a provider for hosts and van operators.
  • Ratings on both sides, Super Host rules, and automatic flags for too many cancellations or disputes.
  • Personal data follows India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: collect only what is needed, get consent, allow deletion.

Charging van dispatch

  • Vans are supply that moves, like Uber drivers. Each van reports location and remaining battery; dispatch picks the nearest van with enough energy.
  • Pricing: fixed call-out fee plus per-kWh energy. The van partner keeps 80%, VoltPool 20%.
  • Start with one partner and one or two vans in a single area, at fixed hours.

9. Unit economics and risks

Car session: 15 kWh × ₹16/kWh = ₹240 platform 15% = ₹36 host gets ₹204 Host's power cost ≈ 15 × ₹9 = ₹135 → host profit ≈ ₹69 Peak session: same car at 7 pm: ₹19/kWh → ₹285, platform ₹43 Scooter: 2 kWh × ₹13/kWh = ₹26 platform 15% = ₹3.9 Van rescue: ₹199 call-out + 6 kWh × ₹22 = ₹331 platform 20% = ₹66 Driver saving: 15 kWh ≈ 105 km in a car; petrol for 105 km ≈ ₹600 vs ₹240 One locality: 40 hosts × 3 sessions/day × ₹20 avg fee ≈ ₹72,000 / month

Later revenue lines: a monthly driver pass with lower prices, featured listings for shop hosts, and B2B plans for delivery and e-auto operators who need daily top-ups.

Regulation to check early: India's Ministry of Power guidelines treat EV charging as a service and allow it without a licence, and many DISCOMs offer a separate EV tariff. Whether a host may charge strangers from a normal domestic meter varies by state and DISCOM. In the pitch, say "hosts use an EV-tariff connection or a sub-meter", and verify with your local DISCOM before a real launch. A prepaid wallet also falls under RBI rules, so use a licensed partner's wallet.
  • Cold start: launch in one area (a college campus or one locality), sign up 15–20 shop hosts by hand, and target delivery riders and e-autos first because they charge every day.
  • Safety: require an earthed socket or certified charger, and add host insurance later.
  • Leakage: keep both sides on the platform with booking guarantees, payment protection, ratings, wallet credits and promos.
  • Disputes: the start PIN plus timestamps for every booking state give a clear record; move busy hosts to smart plugs early.

10. How to demo it to judges

  1. In the Driver app at 18% battery, tap Charge now, show the best match and "next option". Point out that prices shown already include time of day and demand.
  2. Open Ravi Auto Spares: photo gallery, technical details, amenities. Choose Offer your price, slide to ₹13 and send.
  3. Switch to the Host app (the badge shows the new offer). Counter at ₹15. Switch back and accept the counter in the Driver app.
  4. Start navigation; the Host app shows "Aarav is on the way". Skip to arrival, take a check-in photo, and read the PIN.
  5. In the Host app, type the PIN to start charging and show kWh and earnings ticking live. Stop the session; both bells show the payment and earnings.
  6. Show the receipt in the Driver app (fee, host payout, petrol saving) and rate with a photo.
  7. In the Host app: post a new ad with the camera, raise the night price on Pricing, and activate BOOST24 on Offers. Back in the Driver app, the new ad is featured and the coupons tab shows what is live now.
  8. End on Admin with the revenue chart, and mention the charging van for nearly empty batteries.
Figures are planning estimates for India (late 2026) and should be checked against your city's tariffs and fuel prices before the final pitch.
